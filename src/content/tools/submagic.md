## What it is

Submagic is a browser-based AI editor for short vertical video. You upload a clip or paste a YouTube link, and it adds animated captions, removes silences and bad takes, adds zooms, B-roll, and sound effects, and exports a video formatted for TikTok, Instagram Reels, and YouTube Shorts. Instead of a timeline, you mostly edit by changing the transcript.

Its Magic Clips feature takes a long video, such as a podcast or interview, picks out key moments, and turns them into several short clips. Newer additions cover the rest of the job: scheduling and publishing straight to social platforms, an analytics dashboard, AI avatars, and a "Find Ideas" tool that suggests what to post next.

Submagic is based in Paris. David Zitoun and Tsi-fei Chan founded it in 2023. Zitoun has said publicly that they started with $500 and no funding. In February 2026 he described it on LinkedIn as a "15 person remote team" that is bootstrapped. The site says it has more than 4 million users.

## Who it's for

Submagic fits people who already film themselves and want captioned, punchy shorts without learning a pro editor. Think coaches, consultants, real estate agents, local businesses, podcasters cutting clips, and creators posting several Reels or Shorts a week. Business plans add more seats and publishing accounts for small agencies and marketing teams.

It's a weaker fit if you need a full editor with multi-track timelines, color grading, or detailed audio mixing. It also has no native mobile app. You can upload from a phone browser, but its help center says to edit on a computer.

## Standout details

- **Animated captions in 100+ languages.** Captions are the core feature. You can choose a preset style or, since January 2026, set custom word and emoji animations. An October 2026 update added 52 languages, which Submagic says brings speech recognition to more than 100.
- **Edit by transcript.** You can cut, trim, and remove silences or filler words by editing the text instead of scrubbing through footage.
- **Magic Clips.** Turns one long video into several shorts automatically.
- **AI Auto Edit.** Launched in January 2026. Pick a template, upload a video, and it applies captions, cuts, pacing, and effects in one pass.
- **Rebuilt B-roll library.** B-Rolls 2.0 (April 2026) added more than 10 million clips aimed at short-form content, after users complained the old ones felt too corporate.
- **Publish and track in one place.** Since June 2026 you can schedule and publish to TikTok, Instagram, YouTube, LinkedIn, X, and Facebook and see views, engagement, and follower growth in one dashboard. Publishing is on Pro and Business only.
- **API and MCP server.** A public API is on every plan, and an MCP server (May 2026) lets an AI assistant such as Claude create projects, generate clips, and publish for you.
- **Frequent updates.** The public changelog shows new features almost every month in 2026.

## Pricing

Submagic is a paid product with a free trial. Prices are per member, from the [pricing page](https://www.submagic.co/pricing) and help center as of November 12, 2026. Yearly billing saves up to 41%.

- **Free trial: $0.** No credit card. Upload and edit 3 short videos. To download without a watermark, you need a paid plan. There is no ongoing free plan.
- **Starter: $19/month, or $12/month billed yearly.** 45 credits a month (about 15 videos), up to 2 minutes per video, AI captions, free B-roll and audio, transcript trimming, 1080p at 30 FPS, no watermark.
- **Pro: $39/month, or $23/month billed yearly.** 120 credits (about 40 videos), up to 5 minutes per video, Storyblocks B-roll and audio, auto zooms, removal of silences and bad takes, audio cleanup, social publishing and analytics for one profile per platform, brand kit, and export up to 2K.
- **Business: $69/month, or $41/month billed yearly.** 300 credits (about 100 videos), up to 30 minutes per video, 4K at 60 FPS, custom templates, logos and a custom words dictionary, priority support and rendering, 100 API minutes a month, and three publishing profiles per platform.
- **Custom:** through sales, with SSO and custom limits.

How credits work: uploading a video costs 3 credits, and captions are free. Each extra AI feature costs 1 to 3 credits, and each Magic Clip costs 8. Credits reset monthly, and you can buy more from your account. Submagic's help center says it does not offer refunds.

## Honest verdict

Submagic is one of the quickest ways to turn raw talking-head footage into captioned shorts that look professionally edited, and it keeps adding features at a fast pace. G2 reviewers often praise its ease of use and time savings. The catches are in billing and reliability: per-member credits, a maximum video length on each plan, and no refunds. Reddit and G2 also include some sharp complaints about glitchy exports and downtime. Use all three trial videos on your own typical footage, then start on monthly billing before you commit to a year.

### Pros

- Caption styles look polished out of the box, and they're quick to apply.
- Editing by transcript and removing silences in one click save real time on talking-head videos.
- One tool covers editing, clipping, scheduling, and publishing to six platforms.
- Supports 100+ languages for speech recognition.
- Bootstrapped, founder-led team that ships updates nearly every month.
- API on every plan, plus an MCP server for automating work through AI assistants.

### Cons

- No free plan. The trial gives you 3 videos, and its downloads are watermarked.
- Credits reset every month, and AI features use them up faster than captions alone. On Reddit, a user said leftover credits were wiped when their plan renewed.
- Starter caps videos at 2 minutes. Auto zooms, silence removal, and publishing need Pro, and 4K needs Business.
- Every seat costs extra, so teams add up fast.
- The help center says Submagic does not offer refunds.
- Some Reddit and G2 reviewers report glitchy or out-of-sync exports, downtime, and weak Magic Clips on dense content. One Reddit user said failed projects still used up credits. Others say support fixed their issues quickly.
- Not a full editor: no multi-track timeline or color grading, and no native mobile app.
